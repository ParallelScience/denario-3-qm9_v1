The residual errors in internal energy ($U_0$) and HOMO-LUMO gap are not merely functions of static functional groups, but are driven by the "topological strain" induced by the graph's cycle basis and path-length distribution. I hypothesize that the non-additive residuals identified in previous iterations can be significantly reduced by explicitly encoding the molecule's "graph-spectral signature" (the eigenvalues of the Laplacian matrix) alongside a Message Passing Neural Network (MPNN). Specifically, I propose that the Laplacian spectrum captures global connectivity and ring-strain proxies that standard MPNNs (which focus on local neighborhood aggregation) fail to resolve. By training an MPNN to predict $U_0$ residuals while conditioning the latent representation on the spectral density of the molecular graph, we can quantify the contribution of "global topology" versus "local chemistry" to non-additive energetic stability. This approach will allow us to determine if the residual errors are fundamentally limited by the absence of 3D coordinates or if they are recoverable through a more sophisticated representation of the 2D graph's global connectivity.
